The Steps of Our Verification Process

Our comprehensive verification process involves several key stages, each designed to ensure the integrity and accuracy of the reviews we publish:

  • Initial Submission Review: Every review submitted to Verified-Psychic-Reviews.net first undergoes an initial screening. We look for basic compliance with our submission guidelines, including appropriate language, relevance to psychic services, and absence of spam or promotional content. Reviews that appear to be purely promotional or contain offensive language are immediately flagged.
  • Identity Confirmation: A crucial step in our process involves confirming the identity of the reviewer. While we protect reviewer anonymity on the public site, we require internal verification of their email address and, in some cases, may request additional information to confirm they are genuine individuals who have utilized psychic services. This helps prevent fake reviews from bots or competitors.
  • Psychic Service Confirmation: We strive to confirm that the reviewer genuinely engaged with the psychic they are reviewing. This might involve cross-referencing details provided in the review with publicly available information about the psychic, or, in some instances, reaching out to the psychic (with the reviewer’s permission) to confirm a session took place. This step is vital in ensuring the review is based on an actual experience.
  • Content Analysis and Redaction: Our team carefully analyzes the content of each review for consistency, factual claims, and potential biases. We ensure that reviews are constructive and helpful, avoiding personal attacks or unsubstantiated accusations. While we encourage honest feedback, we may redact specific personal details or inflammatory language that does not contribute to the review’s overall value.
  • Publication Decision: After successfully navigating all the above stages, the review is approved for publication on Verified-Psychic-Reviews.net. Our goal is to publish reviews that offer genuine insights, helping others make informed decisions.

What good review verification looks like in general

Review verification is hard to do well, and the phrase is used loosely across the web. Knowing what different levels of checking can and cannot establish helps you read any verification claim sensibly.

Levels of checking

  • Basic screening. Filtering spam, abuse and obviously promotional text. This removes noise but does not prove a review is real.
  • Account checks. Confirming that an email address works or that a user is a person. Useful against bots, but not proof of purchase.
  • Proof of purchase. A receipt, order number or platform-confirmed booking. The strongest common signal, though documents can be edited and privacy limits what can be shared.
  • Pattern analysis. Looking at timing, wording and account overlaps across many reviews to flag coordinated campaigns.

Limits every verification system shares

Even strong checks cannot confirm that a person’s experience was accurately described. They can show that someone used a service, not that a reading was good or that their opinion is fair. Verification also cannot capture people who never review at all, so published feedback can drift towards the very happy and the very angry.

Questions to ask any site that says reviews are verified

  1. What exactly is checked: identity, purchase, both or neither?
  2. Are verified and unverified reviews labelled differently?
  3. Does the site publish negative reviews and explain what gets rejected?
  4. Can providers pay to remove or hide feedback?
  5. Is there a correction route, and a last-updated date?

Frequently asked questions

Is a verified review always true?

No. Verification reduces certain risks, but it does not guarantee honest or representative opinions.

Why might a genuine review be rejected?

It may lack detail, include personal data, breach content rules or look similar to spam. Reasonable sites explain such decisions.
